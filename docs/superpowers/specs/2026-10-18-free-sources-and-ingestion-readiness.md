# Free sources and mature ingestion readiness

Status: plan/research update, 2026-10-18; not an implemented ingestion service. User constraint: **all tracked data sources must be free**. This document narrows the earlier source proposals and is required alongside the [technical architecture](2026-10-18-technical-architecture.md) and [implementation plan](../plans/2026-10-18-data-ingestion.md).

## 1. Free-source policy

No purchased feed, paid API/data subscription, trial that converts to paid access, paywalled article retrieval, or licensed commercial benchmark is required or enabled by this plan. Source registration can be required if it is free. Open-data licences and attribution requirements still apply; free access and permission to retain/reuse/train/redistribute are separate checks.

Registry entries need `access_fee=0`, `requires_paid_subscription=false`, source/terms URLs, terms-check date, attribution, permitted uses, registration/auth method, quotas and `enabled` status. A source changing to paid access is disabled and reported; the importer does not buy access or silently switch providers. Unverified reuse permissions mean metadata-only discovery or a disabled candidate, not acceptance into the training corpus.

Storage, hosting and model API processing are separate costs; free data sources do not make those free. Deterministic parsing, manual review and CPU baselines must work without a paid LLM. Paid extraction remains optional and disabled by default. Local processing and existing disks can avoid service bills, but large remote storage/continuous hosting may eventually cost money. Do not promise unlimited free infrastructure.

Yahoo/yfinance, paid publishers and proprietary commodity/carbon/freight benchmarks are **not tracked dependencies** in this free-source plan. Existing financial demo code may stay isolated, but no acceptance/training gate requires it. Voluntary user-owned supplier quotes or delivery records can be imported without buying a feed if their permissions and field definitions are clear; their absence remains a ground-truth gap.

Important free-data limits: exact delivered-to-site quotes, supplier lead times, complete historical business news and some spot/futures benchmarks may not have suitable free coverage. Use explicitly named official indices/proxies or show unavailable. Never invent prices, company exposures, delays or historical release dates to fill those gaps.

Target onboarding must also lock VAT/tax treatment, building/work category and the provider's reference-period convention. A quarterly construction-price survey may refer to a representative month rather than a quarterly average. Preserve that definition and document the mapping to forecast quarters; never average/relabel periods silently.

## 3. Recommended long-term storage approach

Keep **durable, versioned data outside the training graph**, then build both graph and model inputs from that data:

1. **Evidence archive:** immutable source bytes plus request/release metadata, content hashes, permissions and parser versions, stored in private object storage or an existing disk. Corrections produce new versions. This lets future parsers/extractors improve without repeatedly relying on a mutable source website.
2. **Canonical records:** typed entities/aliases, scoped relation facts, series definitions and append-only observations/revisions, joined by stable IDs. Keep publication/availability, reference/event and ingestion/commit times separate. An operational database's MVCC or table snapshots do not automatically provide economic-vintage history; preserve it explicitly.
3. **Derived graph:** rebuildable node/edge projections selected at a cutoff, not an independent competing truth source. Histories remain shared tables linked by IDs. Add a native graph server only for a demonstrated traversal workload.
4. **Training releases:** immutable, checksummed Parquet/Arrow feature/label partitions plus sparse graph tensors, masks, entity maps, splits and transforms. Pin the source/canonical versions in a manifest. Train from a frozen release, never from whatever the dashboard happens to show today.
5. **Model artifacts:** separate checkpoints, forecasts, evaluation/calibration reports and lineage back to the training release. Predictions are not measurements and cannot enter targets as if observed.

For this single-worker project, existing **LanceDB + SQLite + object storage** implements that separation with the least disruption. Parquet partitions and manifests keep the valuable data portable, regardless of the next database choice.

For a shared long-running service with independent concurrent writers/users, my preferred mature operational backend is **PostgreSQL** for canonical identities/facts/histories, reviews and jobs, plus the same object archive and frozen training releases. Its transactions/constraints and concurrent access support make that a sensible operational destination. LanceDB can then be a derived optional vector index, rather than another canonical write target. [PostgreSQL concurrency](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/mvcc-intro.html). Timescale/PostGIS and a graph server are later workload-specific choices, not prerequisites.

This is a staged recommendation, not an instruction to migrate now. Migrate only when measured single-worker limits or shared-service requirements justify it. Preserve canonical IDs/version semantics, export and reconcile all history/tombstones, test identical as-of/graph/dataset outputs, take a verified backup, then switch the single authoritative writer. Do not implement permanent canonical dual writes. Hosting costs remain separate from free data access.

## 4. What makes ingestion mature

The earlier plan already contains the main implementation tasks. These are the release gates that distinguish a functioning scraper from dependable ingestion:

| Gate | Required evidence | Owning tasks |
|---|---|---|
| Source contract and free access | Exact endpoint/series definition, zero data-access fee, permitted use/attribution, quotas and auth tested; no trial/subscription fallback. Candidate sources remain disabled until checked. | 1, 4A, 7 |
| Recoverable/idempotent imports | Duplicate replay, out-of-order arrivals, retries and crashes preserve one committed result; staged records are invisible and cursors lose no items. | 2, 3, 4, 8 |
| Trustworthy values and scope | Evidence spans/cells, units, price definition, product/geography, missing values, legal conditions and reviewed labels pass the design's quality gates. Unknowns stay unknown. | 2, 5, 6, 7 |
| Historical correctness | Eligible revisions, corrections/retractions and source release dates reconstruct past snapshots; a future perturbation cannot change old graph/features. Missing vintages are declared. | 3, 7, 10 |
| Source-specific monitoring | Each enabled source records expected release/update cadence, allowable delay, last success/cursor, parse/accept/review counts, revision/schema changes and alert owner. Quiet news is distinguished from a failed fetch/parser. | 1, 8, 9 |
| Incident and schema handling | Registry-defined contract breaches quarantine new records and alert/status; raw evidence and last accepted data remain intact. Runbook covers auth failures, changed units/base years, removed series, endpoint migrations, source becoming paid and disk exhaustion; schema/parser fixes can replay saved evidence. | 4, 7, 9 |
| Resource safety and restore | Storage/temporary limits and optional model budgets enforced; remote checksums, manifest-backed backups and isolated restore tested. A seven-day unattended rehearsal is a first operations gate, not proof of all future reliability. | 4, 9 |
| Reproducible training and real evaluation | Frozen dataset/model manifests, independent future outcomes, chronological split, baselines, sample-size report and no synthetic production truth. GNN remains experimental if it cannot improve honest held-out results. | 10, 11 |

Minimum operational rehearsal includes a source timeout, malformed but HTTP-200 response, changed schema/unit, a revised historical value, duplicate syndicated news, a restart mid-publication, a free source becoming paid/unavailable, and restore on an empty temporary instance. Report results per source and keep failed gates open.

Free-source discovery research and plan writing are complete as a proposal. Exact selected series, endpoint/credential smoke tests, ongoing terms checks, source-vintage coverage and these maturity gates remain implementation work. No ingestion or mature-service readiness is claimed by this document.
